A Broken Rocket Makes A New Hole On Moon

모르는 단어를 누르면 뜻이 나와요

Our Moon has a big new hole on its dry grey face today. Many space rocks make holes on the Moon, but this hole is very different. A heavy piece of space trash fell from the dark sky very fast. It hit the Moon hard, and it made a new hole in the ground.

This story began when a big rocket went up into the sky last year. The strong rocket carried two small Moon landers up into space from our Earth. Then the top part of the rocket was left alone in the cold sky. It did not come back to Earth, so it kept flying far away.

The heavy rocket traveled through deep space for more than one long year. The pull of Earth, the Moon, and the Sun slowly pushed the piece. Space winds from our hot Sun also pushed the big machine toward the Moon. Soon the old rocket was flying straight toward the round white Moon.

The Moon has no air around it, so things do not burn up there. When space rocks fall to Earth, our thick air makes them burn away. But this heavy rocket did not slow down, and it hit the Moon hard. The crash happened very fast, and it threw up a lot of gray dust.

Big eyes on Earth and in space watched the crash site very closely. A huge glass tool in Chile saw a big cloud of dust rise up. The machine saw tiny bits of metal from the rocket and the Moon rocks. This bright dust cloud told us what was inside the hard lunar ground.

A smart ship from South Korea flew over the Moon a few days later. The Korean ship took bright pictures, and people saw the fresh mark right away. But the ship was high up, so the picture was not very clear.

Next, another great space ship took much better pictures of the new crash mark. The picture showed a hole that was eighteen meters wide across the ground. Long lines of broken rocks spread out from the hole like a bright star.

Scientists were very happy because they can learn many things from this big crash. People want to send new travelers to the Moon again very soon. This new hole helps us know how flying space trash can hurt future Moon bases.
